21. TELEMETRY ("SHARE TELEMETRY DATA")

   Telemetry serves three purposes only: finding and fixing crashes and errors, making FMG Client
   run well on the many different PCs it is used on, and keeping the service secure. It is never
   used for advertising, never sold and never passed to third parties other than as described in
   section 22.

   a) On by default, off at any time. "Share telemetry data" is switched on when FMG Client is
      installed. The agreement screen says so, and you can switch it off, or on again, at any time
      under Settings > Launcher > Privacy. FMG Client works exactly the same with telemetry off.
   b) What is collected - and nothing else:
      - About the installation: the versions of the FMG Launcher, the FMG Client mod, the
        S.T.A.L.K.E.R. 2 tab, Minecraft and Fabric Loader; the Windows version, build and language
        setting; the processor model with its cores and threads; memory in whole gigabytes; the
        graphics card model, its video memory and its driver version; the size and scaling of your
        screens; the free space on the drive Minecraft is on; the launcher's settings (the memory
        given to the game, sounds and their volume, the live background, Streamer Mode, Discord
        status, automatic updates); the mods installed for Minecraft (their IDs and versions, and
        whether they are enabled), how many texture packs are enabled and which shader; whether
        S.T.A.L.K.E.R. 2 is installed, its build and branch; and how often FMG Client's features
        are used (plain counts, for example how often Play was pressed or a tab was opened).
      - From the game (FMG Client notes these while you play): which of FMG Client's own mods are
        enabled; the Java version and the graphics driver Minecraft reports; the video settings
        (render distance, frame rate limit, VSync, graphics mode, GUI scale, fullscreen); the
        servers you join - their address and the name you gave them, when, for how long, your
        average and highest ping, and the message shown when you left (servers in your home
        network, servers joined by a plain IP address and worlds shared with e4mc are noted only
        as such, without the address or the name); frame-rate summaries (average frames per
        second, the 1 % lows, the lowest, the number of very slow frames, and how often and how
        long Java paused the game to free memory, with the memory it may use); and errors of FMG
        Client's own mods (which mod, what went wrong and where in its code - at most ten per game
        session).
      - Events: when the launcher and the game start and end, and how long starting took; crashes
        (Minecraft's crash report, or the last part of the game's log when it closed with an
        error); crashes and errors of the launcher (what went wrong, and where in its code);
        downloads that failed or were large (the host, the size and the speed); clip uploads
        (size, speed, the host, whether they worked); clips saved (their length, picture size and
        frame rate, the encoder, the file size, how long saving took, whether sound and the
        microphone were included - never the clip, the picture or the game's name); which emotes
        were played how often and how often the emote wheel was opened (counts); an import from
        other clients (which client, and how many servers, mods and packs were imported - no
        names); which S.T.A.L.K.E.R. 2 build is started.
   c) What is never collected: chats or messages, what you type, keystrokes, screenshots and clips
      themselves, the content of your files, worlds or saves, passwords, sign-in tokens or keys,
      the details of your Microsoft, Steam, Nexus Mods or Discord accounts, your browsing history,
      or other programs on your PC. Before anything is sent, the launcher removes from crash
      reports and logs the name of your Windows user and of your PC, the folders of your user
      profile, and anything that looks like a token or a session ID.
   d) Linked to your Minecraft account. While the launcher is signed in to FMG Cosmetics,
      telemetry is linked to your Minecraft account (its UUID and name). The link is made with the
      same sign-in that proves which account you use, so nobody else can send telemetry in your
      name. While the launcher is not signed in, telemetry carries only a random installation ID
      created on your PC. The link exists so that, when you report a crash or a problem, it can be
      examined and fixed for you.
   e) Who sees it. Administrators of FMG Client: as totals across all installations and - when
      examining a crash, a problem a player reported, or a security incident - for a single
      player. Every view of a single player's telemetry is recorded in the security log (section
      20 e).
   f) When it is sent. To the FMG Cosmetics server about one minute after the launcher starts,
      then about every 30 minutes while it runs, and shortly after a game ends. The FMG Client mod
      in the game never sends anything itself: it writes its notes into a folder on your PC
      (fmgclient\telemetry in the game folder) and the launcher sends them. Nothing is sent before
      you have agreed to Part A, and nothing while telemetry is off. What could not be sent yet
      waits on your PC until the next start.
   g) Switching it off. The launcher stops collecting at once, deletes on your PC what was waiting
      to be sent, removes the game's switch so that the mod stops noting anything, and asks the
      server to delete the telemetry of that installation and, when signed in, of your whole
      account. From that moment the server accepts nothing more for it and deletes it 14 days
      later. The delay exists so that a problem you had shortly before can still be examined. If
      you want it deleted sooner, tell us (section 18 b). Switching telemetry on again starts with
      a new installation ID.
   h) Legal basis. The processing of telemetry is based on our legitimate interests in finding
      and fixing crashes and errors, in keeping FMG Client working on different PCs and in keeping
      it secure (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR). You can object at any time, without giving reasons, simply
      by switching telemetry off (section 25). Where storing information on your PC or reading
      information from it requires your consent (Section 25 (1) TDDDG), FMG Client relies on the
      consent you declare by agreeing on the agreement screen, which states that telemetry is on,
      and by leaving "Share telemetry data" switched on; you can withdraw that consent at any time
      with effect for the future by switching it off. Withdrawing consent does not affect the
      lawfulness of what happened before.

   be promised (section 10).
